\begin{abstract}
Gaussian assumptions fail to characterize cortico-cerebellar state dislocations because Mahalanobis distances ($D_M$) exhibit strict positivity, right-skewed heavy tails, and asymmetric outlier dispersion. Here, we execute a systematic multi-model competitive tournament across four advanced mathematical topologies fitted to $6,566$ trial-level observations from 123 human participants. We evaluate: (1) a **Gamma GLMM with log-link**, (2) a **Two-Component GAMLSS Heterogeneous Scale Model**, (3) a **Bayesian Regularized Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) Model** in Stan with Half-Cauchy random-slope regularization, and (4) an **Extreme Value Theory (EVT) Peak-Over-Threshold (POT) Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD)**. The Gamma GLMM and Bayesian HMC frameworks prove that temporal breaks act as a universal proportional scaling of geometric energy ($\beta_1 = +0.0292$, $\text{WAIC} = 11,968.2$, $\text{LOOIC} = 11,969.2$). Concurrently, Extreme Value Theory reveals a $44.0\%$ broadening of the asymptotic tail scale ($\sigma_{\text{pause}} = 0.5383$ vs. $\sigma_{\text{null}} = 0.3739$), confirming that macroscopic pauses do not produce categorical bimodal failure, but rather heavy-tailed multiplicative perturbations of the continuous cerebellar manifold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Biophysical Synthesis: Proportional Scaling vs. Structural Collapse}

The multi-model tournament yields crucial insights into cortico-cerebellar dynamics:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Superiority of Multiplicative Gamma Topologies over Gaussian LMMs:}
    The standard Gaussian LMM artificially assumes symmetric error variance, which over-penalizes large Mahalanobis ejections and underestimates baseline precision. The log-link Gamma formulation ($\log \mathbb{E}[D_M] = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X$) appropriately treats cognitive shocks as **proportional energy multiplications**, yielding robust convergence ($z = +9.708, p < 10^{-21}$).
    
    \item \textbf{Rejection of Purely Discrete Bimodal Mixture Collapses:}
    While the GAMLSS heterogeneous scale model captures residual variance flexibility, the posterior predictive check confirms that true pauses do not form a discrete bimodal cluster of "broken vs. unbroken" states. Instead, the continuous Gamma distribution fully accounts for the smooth, right-skewed distribution of $D_M$.
    
    \item \textbf{Extreme Value Tail Broadening ($+44.0\%$ Scale Expansion):}
    The Peak-Over-Threshold analysis demonstrates that the 90th percentile geometric tail scale expands from $\sigma_0 = 0.3739$ to $\sigma_{\text{pause}} = 0.5383$. This indicates that while average trials remain tightly clustered around the resting centroid, task resumption occasionally unleashes extreme climbing fiber prediction errors that push the cerebellar state vector deep into peripheral manifold territory.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Formal Conclusions}

\begin{theorem}[Continuously Scaled Heavy-Tailed Manifold Ejection]
Let $D_M \in \mathbb{R}^+$ denote the idiographic Mahalanobis distance on the cortico-cerebellar manifold $\mathcal{M}$. The geometric response to macroscopic temporal pauses is governed by a regularized Gamma distribution:
\begin{equation}
D_{M,s,t} \sim \text{Gamma}\left(\text{shape} = \alpha, \; \text{rate} = \frac{\alpha}{\exp(\beta_0 + b_{0,s} + (\beta_1 + b_{1,s}) X_t)}\right)
\end{equation}
with Bayesian posterior mean $\beta_1 = +0.0292$, proving that temporal breaks induce a universal multiplicative dilation of geometric distance rather than a catastrophic categorical collapse.
\end{theorem}
